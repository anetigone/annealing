\section{Switch Readiness and Budget-Matched Controls}
\label{sec:switch_ready}

\subsection{A terminal preimage as a switching target}
The intermediate prefix need not reach the terminal rectangle itself.
Let $\mathcal B_1$ be the class-1 backprojection box on $E$, and let
$\delta$ bound each coordinate of the update error.
A switch-ready set $S$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ready_preimage}
 T_{v_L}(S;b)+[-\delta,\delta]^2\subseteq G_1
 \quad\text{for every }b\in\mathcal B_1.
\end{equation}
This is an inner approximation to the intersection of one-step
terminal preimages. If a prefix reaches $S$ in $m$ calls, the first
terminal call gives entry into $G_1$ by call $m+1$. Subsequent
terminal invariance and error estimates apply after this entry.
The extra call must be counted when comparing entrance budgets.

\begin{proposition}[Order envelopes for switch readiness]
\label{prop:ready_envelope}
For the aligned three-template model with $a,d>0$, assume
$c(v)\ge0$ and $\gamma(B_{ii}+c(v))\le1$ on a closed band $I$.
Use the order
$(s,t)\preceq(s',t')$ when $s\le s'$ and $t\ge t'$.
Let $b^-\preceq b\preceq b^+$ cover $\mathcal B_1$, and put
$\zeta^-=(-\delta,\delta)$, $\zeta^+=(\delta,-\delta)$.
Starting with $L_0=b^-$ and $U_0=b^+$, choose envelopes satisfying,
for every $v\in I$,
\begin{align}
 L_{k+1}&\preceq T_v(L_k;b^-)+\zeta^-,\nonumber\\
 T_v(U_k;b^+)+\zeta^+&\preceq U_{k+1}.\label{eq:ready_envelope}
\end{align}
If the order interval $[L_m,U_m]_{\preceq}$ is contained in $S$
and~\eqref{eq:ready_preimage} holds, every allowed prefix enters
$G_1$ after one terminal call. Each prefix variance can depend on
the observation and the entire previous history.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The posterior covariance satisfies
\[
 C_{12}=-d w_2\{a(1-w_2)+w_1\}\le0.
\]
Thus the off-diagonal map derivatives are nonpositive, while
$\partial_iT_{v,i}=1-\gamma(B_{ii}+c(v))+\gamma c(v)C_{ii}/v\ge0$.
The map is order preserving in both the state and the
backprojection, which enters additively as $\gamma b$.
If $L_k\preceq x_k\preceq U_k$, applying these monotonicities
and the error endpoints gives the bounds in~\eqref{eq:ready_envelope}
for the actual $v_k$. Induction gives the prefix enclosure,
including arbitrary band choices at every call. Its inclusion in
$S$, followed by~\eqref{eq:ready_preimage}, proves terminal entry.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Certified bands at the selected geometries}
The diagnostic uses $a=1$, $r=1.29$, $\alpha=10^{-3}$,
$\tau=\nu=10^{-6}$, $\lambda=1+\alpha^2$, $\gamma=1/4$,
and $v_L=\alpha^2+\alpha^4$. The observation tolerances are
$(\alpha/4,.005)$ and $\delta=10^{-4}$.
For
\[
 S=[.50001,.65]\times[-.02,.05],
 \qquad G_1=[.53,1.1]\times[-.1,.15],
\]
the terminal image, including the observation and update errors,
is enclosed by
$[.62496,.73776]\times[-.01136,.02636]$ on all 16 checked models.
The grid is
$d\in\{1.30,1.43,1.62,1.85\}$ and
$h\in\{1.55,2.05,2.45,3.0\}$.

Candidate centers range from $.24$ to $1.10$ at spacing $.02$,
with total-variance width $.02$ and a 25-call entrance cap.
Each whole band is covered by five closed subintervals;
45-digit outward-rounded odds bounds produce the envelopes.
These bounds cover the continuous variance band, rather than
only the sampled center or endpoint trajectories.

The original all-class terminal conditions pass on 12 models.
For these models the ready prefixes use 13--18 intermediate calls
and guarantee terminal entry in 14--19 total calls. At each
ready center, the identical band and enclosure tool require
16--21 calls to certify direct entry into $G_1$.
Switching earlier saves three intermediate calls on nine models
and two on three models; after counting the terminal entry call,
the sufficient entrance budgets improve by two and one,
respectively. This compares sufficient certified budgets, not
optimal actual hitting times.

The four models at $d=1.30$ retain their original excluded status:
their class-2 rectangle is not terminal invariant. A later
narrower rectangle restores the checks, but is a separate
post hoc result. None of these model-wise bands enlarges the
continuous geometry box of Theorem~\ref{thm:ind_noise}.
The fixed entry-error margin also does not replace the vanishing
terminal-error assumptions in its asymptotic risk statement.

\subsection{Fair controls and independent noise validation}
The first short-budget comparison reused 576 observations on the
12 selected models. Its shared platform had been selected for
106 calls. A follow-up minimizes noiseless proxy error separately
at 21, 25, 32, 40 and 106 calls and adds the same-center direct-entry
control above. At 21 calls the ready, direct and retuned shared
errors are $.00347145$, $.01535845$ and $.00711382$.
The ready rule improves all-class error on 12/12 models at each
short budget; at 106 calls the three errors are about
$1.43\cdot10^{-12}$. These follow-up controls reuse observations
and are not independent confirmation.

The independent validation freezes these 12 geometries and both
certified prescriptions. It adds platforms tuned using noisy
calibration observations, with either a shared prefix or the
same observation gate as the ready rule. Both search
$v\in\{.20,.21,\ldots,1.10\}$ and
$m=0,\ldots,\min(25,K-1)$, independently at each budget.
The terminal-only candidate appears once. Selection minimizes
prior-weighted calibration squared error, with ties resolved
by shorter prefix and then smaller variance. The gate reads
only $b_2\le1$ and $b_1>\alpha/2$; evaluation labels never
choose a sample-specific prescription.

Calibration uses 16 draws per class, model and noise setting;
evaluation uses 32 new draws per class. The measurement noise
standard deviations are $10^{-6}$, $.001$ and $.005$,
with $\tau$ and the solver fixed. Separate SeedSequence streams
use phase seeds 2026111601 and 2026111602, followed by noise
and model indices. Choices are saved before evaluation.
All observations, including those outside $E$, enter the risk.
The budgets are 22, 25, 32, 40 and 106. The first readout is
22 rather than the historical 21 because one frozen direct
prefix uses 21 calls; every policy now executes at least one
terminal call before every reported endpoint.

\paragraph{Nominal noise and the same-gate control.}
At $\nu=10^{-6}$, all 1152 new observations lie in $E$.
The ready rule improves all-class error on 12/12 models relative
to both direct entry and noisy tuned sharing at 22, 25, 32 and 40
calls. The noisy tuned gated control instead improves over ready
on 12/12 models at 22, 25 and 32 calls, and 11/12 at 40 calls
(one difference is below $10^{-10}$).
At 22 calls all four policies classify 1152/1152 nearest templates
correctly, whereas their radius-$.1$ neighborhood counts are
1056, 864, 1024 and 1088, respectively.
This is an error and neighborhood improvement, not a mode-count
improvement. At 106 calls all four have 1152/1152 neighborhood
success and error about $1.56\cdot10^{-12}$. Their output RMS
distances from ready are at most $1.49\cdot10^{-11}$.
The tuned gated result rules out interpreting the certified
ready prescription as optimal within these candidate controls.

\paragraph{Noise adaptation and finite calibration.}
At $\nu=.001$, all evaluation observations again lie in $E$.
The short-budget comparisons have the same direction as at
nominal noise. At 106 calls the noisy tuned shared policy
misses two nearest templates on one model, while the ready,
direct and tuned gated policies recover 1152/1152.
The common-gate policies have error $2.53\cdot10^{-7}$.
At $\nu=.005$, only 667/1152 observations lie in $E$, and
the classifier is correct on 1151/1152. The ready rule misses
that one classification and recovers the other 1151; its error
at 106 calls is $.000880549$.
The noisy tuned shared and gated policies give 1136/1152 and
1147/1152 nearest-template successes, respectively.
At 22 calls the tuned gated policy beats ready on ten models
and loses on two, although its mean error is larger.
The short-budget exceptions do not coincide with the two models
responsible for the excess long-budget gated failures.
This pattern does not establish a uniform
noise-robustness advantage.

The calibration sample has only 16 observations per class.
An empirical minimizer can fail on unseen noise, and near-flat
106-call scores can choose different prefixes based on tiny
differences. These results do not establish failure of every
shared or gated platform, or failure after more calibration.
At 106 calls, small terminal field norms coexist with different
errors on failed observations; numerical stationarity alone
does not certify an accurate reconstruction.
The paired Monte Carlo summaries are conditional on these fixed
models and calibration choices. They do not measure uncertainty
over other geometries or image populations.
